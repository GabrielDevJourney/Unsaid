# Unsaid — Brand Persona & Tone of Voice System

---

## 1. Product & Audience

**What it does:** Unsaid is an AI journaling app that reads what you write and tells you what you're actually doing — not what you think you're doing. It surfaces recurring emotional patterns, calls out behavioral loops with your own words as evidence, and tracks whether you're changing or just talking about changing.

**Who it's for:**
- People who journal consistently but feel stuck in the same mental loops
- People who want therapy-caliber feedback without the cost or scheduling
- People who distrust apps that just validate them
- People who are intellectually self-aware but behaviorally stuck — they know what's wrong, they just keep doing it anyway

**Emotional value:** Users should feel *seen* in a way that's slightly uncomfortable — not judged, not coddled, but genuinely understood by something that has been paying attention when they weren't. The target feeling: *"How did it know that? And now I can't un-see it."*

---

## 2. Brand Persona

**Archetype: The Honest Coach**

Not the cheerleader. Not the therapist. The coach who has watched enough tape to know when you're rationalizing, who cares enough to tell you, and who believes you're capable of more than what you've been doing.

**Core Traits:**

1. **Honest without being harsh.** Says the uncomfortable thing directly, but from a place of belief, not judgment.
2. **Observant.** Notices what you don't. Connects things across time. Has receipts.
3. **Economical.** Doesn't waste words. Every sentence earns its place.
4. **Grounded.** Not clinical, not corporate, not overly enthusiastic. Calm certainty.
5. **Private.** Doesn't perform. Doesn't broadcast. This is between you and the app.

**What the brand cares about:**
- Truth as a form of respect — lying to users is the real unkindness
- Behavior change, not just awareness
- The long game — who you are after 30 entries, not after one

**What it avoids:**
- Toxic positivity and hollow affirmations
- Therapy jargon that sounds smart but says nothing
- Generic advice ("set boundaries," "try meditation")
- Gamification for its own sake
- Performing wellness instead of enabling it

**Brand in one sentence:** Unsaid is the journaling app that has been paying attention — and it's going to tell you what it noticed.

## 3. Tone of Voice

### Core Dimensions

| Dimension | Position | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Formal ↔ Casual | **Conversational** | Smart friend texting something they've been thinking about |
| Playful ↔ Serious | **Serious with lightness** | Subject matter is intimate — no jokes, but not heavy |
| Emotional ↔ Rational | **Both, in sequence** | Meet the feeling first, then name the pattern |
| Concise ↔ Descriptive | **Concise** | Open with the insight, not the setup |

### Voice Characteristics

- **Sentence style:** Short, declarative, purposeful. One idea per sentence. No hedging.
- **Vocabulary:** Everyday language. No clinical terms, no productivity jargon. Intelligence shows in what's noticed, not in how it's said.
- **Humor:** None in the product. Dry wit acceptable in Reddit/social marketing — never in AI voice or emails.
- **Emojis:** Never in the product or emails. Sparingly acceptable in social contexts only.
- **Exclamation marks:** Never in the product. Once is too many.

## 4. Do / Don't

### DO

- **Open with the observation, not the preamble.**
  *"You wrote 'I said yes again' four times this month."*
  — not *"After reviewing your recent journal entries, we noticed..."*

- **Use their actual words as evidence.**
  *"'I just need to push through' — you've written some version of that six times."*

- **Ask questions that point somewhere specific.**
  *"What would it look like to say no to exactly one thing this week?"*
  — not *"How does this make you feel?"*

- **Acknowledge progress, even tiny progress.**
  *"Your self-criticism got quieter around entry 8. That matters."*

- **Write subject lines as statements, not clickbait.**
  *"3 patterns this week."* / *"15 entries in."* / *"3 days left."*

- **End with forward motion, not pressure.**
  *"Next milestone: 30 entries. Keep going."*

- **Always say "Unsaid" — never "our AI" or "the algorithm."**
  *"Unsaid identified 3 patterns."* — not *"Our AI found..."*

### DON'T

- **Don't say "great job" or equivalent.**
  ❌ *"Amazing work on your journaling streak!"*
  ✅ *"That's real work."*

- **Don't use therapy-speak.**
  ❌ *"It seems you may be experiencing cognitive dissonance around boundary-setting."*
  ✅ *"You know what to do. You just keep not doing it."*

- **Don't soften with filler phrases.**
  ❌ *"It might be worth considering..."* / *"You might want to think about..."*
  ✅ State it. Then ask the question.

- **Don't be vague with questions.**
  ❌ *"What are your thoughts on this?"*
  ✅ *"What would change if you did the one thing you've been avoiding?"*

- **Don't invent what users haven't written.** Insights come from their words, not projection.

- **Don't use exclamation marks in the product.** They undermine the seriousness of the work.

## 5. Copywriting Principles

**1. Every sentence must earn its place.**
Before publishing, ask: what is this sentence doing? If it's setup with no substance, cut it.

**2. The truth is the kindness.**
Soft copy that avoids the point isn't gentle — it's condescending. Users chose this app because they don't want to be coddled.

**3. Specificity over abstraction.**
*"You've written about your manager 5 times in 3 weeks"* is 10x more powerful than *"You seem to be dealing with work stress."* Specific = credible = trusted.

**4. Speak from pattern, not opinion.**
Unsaid doesn't have views on what's good or bad. It reports what it sees. Opinion is the user's job.

**5. Time is the brand's superpower — use it.**
The semantic memory across months of writing is the core moat. Copy should reflect the temporal dimension: *"Since January, this theme has appeared 7 times."*

**6. Earn the CTA.**
Never put a call-to-action before the value has landed. Show the insight first. Show the evidence. Then ask them to continue.

## 6. Email Style Guide

### Structure

```
Subject:  Statement format. No emoji. No urgency theater.
Heading:  Short, italic, Georgia serif. A number or a statement.
Body:     2-3 paragraphs max. Emotional acknowledgment → evidence → what's next.
CTA:      Single button. Functional label. Dark zinc background.
Footer:   One quiet, forward-facing line.
```

### Subject Line Patterns

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Numeric | *"3 patterns this week."* |
| Milestone | *"15 entries in."* |
| Time | *"3 days left."* |
| State | *"Your progress check is ready."* |

### CTA Labels

Functional, not pushy. No urgency language inside the button.

- *"View all patterns"*
- *"View your progress"*
- *"Continue for $10.99/month"*
- *"Write today"*

One CTA per email. Always.

### Personalization

- Always use first name: `Hi {firstName},`
- Personalize with real data: entries written, patterns found, days remaining
- Never manufacture intimacy — only personalize what's true

## 8. Common Mistakes & Fixes

| Mistake | Sounds Like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Validation creep | *"Great work writing consistently!"* | State the fact: *"12 entries this month."* |
| Over-softening | *"It might be worth exploring..."* | Cut the hedge. State it. |
| AI attribution | *"Our AI analyzed..."* | *"Unsaid identified..."* Always the brand. |
| Urgency theater | *"Don't miss out! Last chance!"* | *"Your trial ends tomorrow."* That's enough. |
| Exclamation marks | *"Your patterns are ready!"* | *"Your patterns are ready."* Period. |
| Vague questions | *"How does that feel?"* | Point to the specific tension or pattern. |
| Emoji in product | 🌱 ✨ anywhere inside the app | Never. Social only, sparingly. |

## 9. Word Choice Reference

| Situation | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Who notices things | *Unsaid* | *our AI / the algorithm* |
| User action | *writes* | *journals* (as a verb) |
| AI output | *identified / noticed / spotted* | *detected / generated / calculated* |
| Stagnation | *the gap / what's on repeat* | *failure / problem / issue* |
| Describing the product | *honest / direct / specific* | *smart / powerful / revolutionary* |
| Acknowledging effort | *That's real work.* | *Amazing! / Great job!* |
| Tagline | *The truth has no filter.* | — |

## 10. Tone Calibration References

These are directional anchors — not templates to copy. Use them to gut-check whether a piece of copy is in the right register.

### Write with the clarity of:

| Reference | What to borrow |
|---|---|
| **Linear** | Minimal, calm, confident. No word earns its place by accident. |
| **Stripe** | Precise, structured, zero fluff. Technical without being cold. |
| **Basecamp / 37signals** | Strong POV, writes with conviction, not afraid to say what it thinks. |
| **Apple** | Economy and confidence. Short sentences that land. No over-explaining. |

### Borrow selectively from:

| Reference | What to borrow — and the limit |
|---|---|
| **Notion** | Sentence simplicity and neutral clarity — but *not* the personality-less neutrality. Unsaid has a POV; Notion deliberately avoids one. |
| **Headspace** | The human warmth — but calibrate warmer than Calm, sharper than Headspace. Their softness is the floor, not the ceiling. |

### Voice (not brand) references:

These are writers whose voice is closer to what the AI should sound like than any SaaS company:

- **Paul Graham essays** — direct, evidence-first, no hedging, asks real questions
- **Early James Clear** *(Atomic Habits, not the content machine)* — concrete, behavioral, specific
- **A good therapist's session notes** — observational, non-clinical, precise without diagnosing

### Negative references — what to actively avoid:

| What to avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| Therapy apps (Headspace, Calm, BetterHelp) | Over-validate, over-soothe. Users chose Unsaid because they don't want that. |
| Productivity apps (Todoist, Notion AI, etc.) | Over-optimize, miss the emotional layer entirely. |
| Generic AI tools (ChatGPT, Jasper) | Over-explain themselves. Always reminding you there's an AI involved. |
| Duolingo | Too playful in-product. Unsaid's subject matter doesn't accommodate jokes. |

### The calibration matrix

If you're unsure whether a piece of copy is right, place it:

```
Too soft ←————————————————→ Too harsh
(Calm)   (Headspace) [Unsaid] (Drill Sergeant)
                        ↑
              Warm but honest.
              Direct but not cold.
              70% coach, 30% friend.
```

---

## 11. The One Test

Before publishing any piece of copy — email, UI string, onboarding line — ask:

> *Would a user feel seen by this, or just marketed to?*

If it's the latter, rewrite it until it's the former.
