## THE CONTENT PHILOSOPHY

**Old approach (wrong):** Post about Unsaid features. Post about what shipped. Post about the product.

**New approach (right):** Post about the domain — self-improvement, self-discovery, behavioral patterns. Gabriel is the protagonist. Unsaid shows up as proof the problem is solvable, not as the subject.

**The sequence that builds an audience:**

1. Person reads a post about a human truth they feel deeply → they follow Gabriel
2. Over time they see him talking about the same domain consistently → they trust him
3. They see a screenshot of the product doing something real → they click
4. They sign up because Gabriel already earned the trust

The product is never the first thing they see. It's the last thing — and by then they already want it.

**The Marc Lou principle:** show the product working, don't describe it. When the product appears, it appears as a screenshot showing the value moment. The copy is minimal. The visual does the work.

## THE THREE CONTENT PILLARS

### Pillar 1 — Domain Truth (50% of posts)
Pure observations about self-awareness, self-improvement, journaling psychology, behavioral patterns.

**No product mention. No CTA. No pitch.**

The reader should feel: "this person understands something about me."

These are the posts that build the following. People follow Gabriel because he says things they've been feeling but couldn't articulate.

**The test:** could this post exist even if Unsaid didn't? If yes, it's a Domain Truth. If no, it's something else.

**What to write about:**
- The gap between knowing what to do and doing it
- Why self-awareness without behavior change is just a comfortable story
- What journaling actually does vs. what people think it does
- The difference between processing an emotion and resolving it
- Why people keep writing the same entry for months
- The uncomfortable thing that happens when you look back at old journals
- Why streaks measure the wrong thing
- The self-improvement industry's core lie

**Example:**
> The self-improvement industry built a business on the feeling of progress.
>
> A good podcast episode feels like growth.
> A finished book feels like growth.
> Writing in your journal feels like growth.
>
> None of them require you to change anything.
>
> The hard question isn't "am I showing up?"
>
> It's "what's different about me compared to six months ago?"

---

### Pillar 2 — Personal Story (30% of posts)
Real moments from Gabriel's own experience with the domain. The story is the post. Unsaid comes up at the end as the natural conclusion — not as a pitch, as the answer he found.

**The structure:** something real that happened → what it revealed → what he did about it

The product shows up in one line, maximum. "So I built something for it" not "so I built Unsaid which does X Y Z."

**The test:** would Gabriel tell this story even if he wasn't building a product? If the story only makes sense as a product pitch, rewrite it.

**What to write about:**
- The moment he went back through journals and saw the same fear written 47 times
- The weeks spent forcing himself to code without AI and what that confusion cost him
- The thing he almost shipped that would have made the product dishonest
- The planning trap — building mental models so detailed that execution feels redundant
- The GT3 RS as a concrete target — not a flex but what happens when you make the destination specific
- Something Catarina pushed back on and why she was right
- The identity confusion between "being a developer" and "being a founder who codes"

**Example:**
> I went back through two years of journals last year.
>
> I'd written about the same fear — over-committing, saying yes when I meant no — 47 times.
>
> Sometimes months apart.
>
> Each time like I was discovering it for the first time.
>
> I wasn't stuck because I didn't know what was wrong.
>

>

**The test:** does the screenshot show Unsaid confronting someone with a truth? If not, don't use it.

## GABRIEL'S VOICE

**What his best posts have in common:**
- Opens with a real moment or specific tension — not a setup
- Short paragraphs. One idea per paragraph
- The insight lives inside the story — never tacked on at the end as a lesson
- Ends with a genuine question he's actually sitting with, not a CTA
- No bullet points in posts. No bold headers. No "Here's what I learned:"
- "I don't know if this is the right call" is more powerful than "Here's the right answer"
- Specific over abstract: "47 times" not "many times", "three months" not "a while"

**His best line so far:** *"How many conversations are we one message away from starting?"* — 1250 impressions. Works because it's a question he was actually asking himself.

**What kills his posts:**
- Structuring them like content ("The Goal:", "The Reason:", numbered takeaways)
- Summarising the insight before letting the reader feel it
- Generic startup language: "excited to announce", "humbled by", "game-changer"
- Any version of "Check out my app"
- Motivation-poster energy
- Writing like a content creator instead of a person with something to say

## THREAD FORMAT

Use for the week's strongest idea. One thread per week max.

**Structure:**
- Tweet 1: Strong observation or tension — the hook
- Tweets 2-3: Evidence or breakdown — why this is true
- Tweet 4: Personal moment — Gabriel's specific experience with this
- Tweet 5: What this led to (product, if earned) — one line
- Tweet 6: Real question — invite conversation

**The rule:** if the thread would work without Tweet 5, the product connection is earning its place. If it only makes sense with Tweet 5, the thread is a product pitch dressed as content. Rewrite it.

## CTA STRATEGY

**Domain Truth posts:** no CTA. Let the post land. The follow is the conversion.

**Personal Story posts:** optional — only "so I built something for it" if the story naturally arrives there. Never force it.

**Product Moment posts:** always one clean line — "byunsaid.com — 7-day free trial" and always in the first reply, never the body. X suppresses reach on posts with external links.

**The rule:** earn the interest first. The CTA is a door, not a pitch. 3 out of 5 posts per week should have no CTA at all.

## FORBIDDEN PATTERNS

**Never say:**
- "I shipped X" / "we shipped X" / "Just shipped"
- "I added a feature where..."
- "We're building" (it's "I'm building" — Gabriel is a founder, not a corporate we)
- "Excited to share" / "Thrilled to announce" / "Humbled by"
- "Check out my app" / "link in bio"
- "Game-changer" / "revolutionary" / "powerful"
- "Journaling app" as self-description (Unsaid is not a journaling app, it's something that confronts you)
- Any developer vocabulary: "the prompt", "the API", "the model", "the schema"

**Never structure a post like:**
- Problem: / Solution: / Lesson:
- Three things I learned:
- A numbered list of insights

**The test before any post:** does this sound like something Gabriel would say to someone at a coffee shop, or does it sound like a content calendar? If it sounds like a calendar, rewrite it.

## POSTS TO NEVER WRITE AGAIN

- "Just shipped [feature] in Unsaid" — the product isn't the news. The problem is the news.
- "Here's what building Unsaid taught me about X" — lesson-first framing kills the story
- "The feature I [almost/did] ship" — if the story is about a feature, it's the wrong story. The story is about what the feature cost to get right.
- Any post that requires knowing what Unsaid is to understand the point
